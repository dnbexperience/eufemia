Each brand's visual identity – logo, colors, typography, imagery and tone of voice – is defined in its brand guidelines. For DNB, that's the Brandbook and its digital version, Brand Center. Eufemia builds on these guidelines for every brand it supports.

Looking for a company or product logo? Browse the [company logos maintained by Eufemia](/collections/logos).

## From brand to product

Brand guidelines describe the identity. Eufemia translates it into something that works in digital products. The same principles apply to every brand:

- **Brand colors become tokens.** Brand colors are kept for identity moments. Functional colors handle interaction, status and surfaces. Both are tested for contrast, so they meet accessibility requirements.
- **Typography is adapted for screens.** Eufemia defines the type scale, line heights and responsive behavior for reading and dense UI.
- **Eufemia fills the gaps.** Spacing, interaction states, iconography, motion and dark mode are rarely covered by brand guidelines. Eufemia defines them in the spirit of each brand.
- **Expressive and functional.** The brand can be loud in campaigns, onboarding and empty states. It stays quiet in forms, tables and settings, where clarity comes first.

## When Eufemia and the brand guidelines differ

Sometimes Eufemia adjusts a brand value, most often a color, to meet accessibility requirements or to work in UI. These adjustments are deliberate and agreed with the brand teams. For digital products, use the Eufemia value.
